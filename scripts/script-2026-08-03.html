Why this matters

Answering services and receptionists cost real money every month. Open-source voice AI finally handles routine calls well. Round trips are under one second now. You keep the audio, the rules, and the savings.

1. Agent Voice Response

This turns any Asterisk phone system into a live AI agent. It is free for personal and commercial use. Callers can interrupt mid-sentence, so it feels human. Swap the speech and language engines freely. Run everything locally with Vosk and Ollama if privacy matters.

2. Dograh AI

Open-source under BSD-2, and built for people who hate code. You drag and drop your call flow visually. It connects to Twilio and Vonage for inbound and outbound calls. Test calls run right in your browser. No phone hardware needed to start.

3. Asterisk AI Voice Agent FX

MIT licensed and aimed at FreePBX users. Its local hybrid mode costs roughly $0.001 to $0.003 per minute. That covers the language model only. You also get warm transfers, voicemail detection, and an admin dashboard.

4. NeMo Realtime On Your Own GPU

Apache-2.0 licensed and speaks the OpenAI Realtime protocol. So you just change one URL and point it home. The full stack fits on a single RTX 4090. That is about 21.5GB of video memory.

5. The Honest Cost Check

Free software does not mean free operations. One FreePBX veteran priced a $6K GPU box realistically. Add power, cooling, and maintenance across three years. Real cost lands near $250 to $300 monthly. Below that usage, cloud APIs are usually cheaper.

Automation tip

Start cloud-only and log every single call for two weeks. Then move the repetitive calls to local models. Route only simple questions to the AI. Send anything unclear straight to a human.
