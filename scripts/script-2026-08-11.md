# Free AI Subtitle Generator: No Watermark, No Subscription

**Hook:** You finally finished your video, then the "free" subtitle tool stamped its logo across it.

## Why this matters

Captions are not optional anymore. Most people scroll with the sound off.
But the tools ranked highest for "free subtitle generator" are often not free at the export step.
They caption your video, then hold the clean file behind a paywall.
Here is what actually exports clean, and where the traps are hiding.

## The trap first: watermark paywalls

VEED adds a watermark to free exports and caps them at 720p.
Removing that watermark starts around twelve dollars per month.
Kapwing also watermarks free exports. Reviewers quote its free caption cap differently, so assume it is small.
Both are decent editors. Just do not expect a publishable file for free.

## 1. Clipchamp: the easiest clean export

Clipchamp is built into Windows 11, and it also runs in a browser.
Auto-captions are free, exports reach 1080p, and there is no watermark.
For most people searching this exact topic, this is the shortest path to a finished file.
Premium stock assets need a paid plan, but your own footage exports clean.

## 2. YouTube Studio: unlimited, with one catch

Upload your video and YouTube generates captions automatically. You edit the transcript right in the browser.
It is unlimited, free, and never watermarked.
The catch is real though. Captions stay a separate track, not burned into the picture.
So it is perfect for YouTube, and useless for a TikTok file you must hand off.

## 3. CapCut: free burned-in captions

CapCut auto-captions are fast, with the word-by-word highlight style short-form viewers expect.
Standard exports carry no watermark. That is the part people get wrong.
The watermark only appears when you use Pro templates or premium assets.
Build your captions with the basic styles and your export stays clean.

## 4. DaVinci Resolve: local AI, zero limits

The free version of Resolve transcribes speech and builds subtitles for you.
All that AI runs locally on your own GPU. No cloud, no account quota, no usage cap.
It never watermarks your exports.
The trade-off is honest: this is professional software, and it wants a reasonably modern GPU.

## 5. Subtitle Edit plus Whisper: the unlimited option

Pick this when the file is too private to upload anywhere.
Subtitle Edit is open source, runs offline, and pairs with Whisper to do the transcription.
You get clean SRT files. No minute cap, no account, no watermark, ever.
It is the least pretty option here, and the hardest one to outgrow.

## Automation tip

Stop captioning one file at a time.
Point Whisper at an entire folder and let it run overnight while you sleep.
You wake up to an SRT file sitting next to every video.
Then one ffmpeg command burns those subtitles into all of them in a single batch.
