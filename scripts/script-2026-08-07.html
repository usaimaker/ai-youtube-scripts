How to Transcribe Audio to Text Free With AI (No Subscription)

"Hook: You have a 45-minute recording, a deadline, and no budget for a transcription subscription."

Hook: You have a 45-minute recording, a deadline, and no budget for a transcription subscription.

Why this matters

Transcripts turn dead audio into searchable notes, subtitles, blog posts, and summaries.

Most guides push paid tools that cap you after a few short minutes.

Every method below is genuinely free, and two of them never upload your audio.

1. Whisper Web (no install, nothing leaves your device)

Whisper Web runs OpenAI's Whisper model inside your browser using WebGPU.

You drag in a file, and the model processes it locally on your own hardware.

No audio reaches a server, so it suits confidential interviews and medical notes.

It is slower on older laptops, roughly two to three times real time.

2. Local Whisper CLI (unlimited, 99 languages)

Install it once with pip install -U openai-whisper, then add ffmpeg for audio decoding.

Run whisper myfile.mp3 --model medium for a solid balance of speed and accuracy.

The model is MIT licensed, has no minute cap, and exports TXT, SRT, and VTT.

The tradeoff is setup time, and speaker labels are not included by default.

3. Word for the Web Transcribe (300 free minutes)

Sign in at office.com with a free Microsoft account, no Microsoft 365 required.

Open Word online, then pick Dictate, choose Transcribe, and upload your audio file.

You get 300 minutes monthly, 200 MB per file, and speaker-labelled output.

This is the easiest option for anyone who refuses to touch a terminal.

4. Riverside Free Transcriber (fastest route to subtitles)

Riverside's free transcriber needs no signup and handles over 100 languages.

Upload your file, then download plain TXT or an SRT caption file.

It is upload-only, so extract the audio first if you start from a link.

5. Otter Free (great tool, one real catch)

Otter's free plan offers 300 minutes monthly with genuinely good speaker labels.

The catch is a lifetime limit of only three uploaded file imports.

Use Otter for live meetings it records itself, not for clearing an old backlog.

Automation tip

Point local Whisper at a folder and loop through every file in one command.

Attach that loop to a nightly scheduled task on your computer.

Your transcripts are waiting each morning, at zero cost and with no upload.
